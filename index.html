01 / Field notes

Problems I've worked

Short write-ups: what went wrong, what the evidence showed, what changed, and how the result was checked.

FN-01AMETEK Sunpower · ESD

Two cryocooler controllers failing ESD immunity testing

Problem
Two cryocooler controller systems had failures under IEC/EN 61000-4-2 ESD testing.
Evidence
Discharged with a 16.5 kV simulator and captured the overcurrent events on an oscilloscope.
Action
Traced each failure to its source and modified the PCB.
Verified
Retested after every change until each controller passed.
FN-02AMETEK Sunpower · Production

A production CryoTel DS30 that failed at startup

Problem
A production cryocooler was failing, and the trigger wasn't known.
Evidence
Isolated the startup conditions that made it fail.
Action
Traced the source with oscilloscope captures.
FN-03Eye Proxy · Enclosure

The CAD model said one thing, the part said another

Problem
Printed enclosure prototypes didn't line up with the purchased parts they were designed around.
Evidence
Measured the actual parts with calipers: the supplier CAD models were dimensionally off. Also found the printer holds about ±0.15 mm, coarser than the tolerances in the model.
Action
Corrected the design to the measured parts. Kept the existing tolerances and accepted a small clearance risk instead of restarting the model.
Result
Three revisions and about ten printed prototypes to an enclosure that holds the board securely.
FN-04Eye Proxy · Bring-up

A reworked module that wouldn't program

Problem
After I hand-soldered a fine-pitch module onto the prototype board, it drew normal current but would not enter programming mode.
Tried
Controlled hot-air reflow at 300–315 °C with 5% airflow to protect neighboring 0201 parts, a second pass with less paste and flux, and an external programmer wired to the reset and boot pins.
Kept moving
Built that subsystem's proof of concept on a development board, which validated the data-rate target and kept firmware work unblocked.
Status
Open. Carried to the next board revision.
FN-05AMETEK Sunpower · Test

358 test procedures, one framework I built from scratch

Built
3,000+ lines of Python. Procedures are defined as JSON data and run by a profile-based menu engine over serial, with an emulator profile for development and the same code on Windows, Linux, and macOS.
Does
Auto-detects board revision, firmware, COM port, and baud rate, validates configuration and temperature-sensor calibration, and logs every result.
Result
Per-procedure time down 95%, fewer production errors, and records that QA, RMA, and MRB reviews could use.

Eye Proxy LLC · Co-founder · Project lead

Leading a four-person hardware team

Eye Proxy is building a vehicle security product. It started as my senior capstone: three engineering students and a faculty advisor, two semesters, from requirements to a working prototype.

  • Ran the weekly meetings and wrote the minutes. Each week everyone named what they would deliver, and action items carried forward with an owner.
  • When something broke, it went on the next agenda, into the minutes, and to someone for follow-up.
  • Managed 32 system-level requirements through SRR, PDR, and CDR.
  • Designed the enclosure (FN-03) and did hands-on bring-up and rework on the first boards (FN-04).

Ohio University Rocket Club · Avionics Lead

A flight logger for half the price of the off-the-shelf one

I led a small team on a short schedule to build a flight controller and payload insert for L1 rockets, built around the SparkFun NINA-B306 and logging continuously to an SD card. It did everything the commercial datalogger we compared against could do, logged faster with more sensors, and cost about half as much.

  • Selected the flight hardware and wrote the firmware; it logged flight data on five L1 launches.
  • Built a Python pipeline to analyze the 200k to 300k sensor readings from each flight.

The next semester I proposed a custom flight-computer board. Our faculty advisor judged it too big a step for the club, so we planned the flight around a commercial Blue Raven instead. That launch was ultimately cancelled.
